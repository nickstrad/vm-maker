# Sources and scope

Research checked on 2026-09-20. These are William Zinsser's own essays in *The American Scholar*.
The skill uses his account of *On Writing Well* and related teaching; it does not claim that every
chapter or edition of the book was reviewed. Guidance is paraphrased; none of the examples in the
skill are quotations from Zinsser.

- [Visions and Revisions](https://theamericanscholar.org/visions-and-revisions/), March 1, 2009.
  Zinsser explains the book's development, its emphasis on clarity and removing clutter, its
  attention to organization, and its room for different personal voices. His discussion of
  trusting the material supports cutting explanations that repeat what the reader can infer.
- [Writing English as a Second Language](https://theamericanscholar.org/writing-english-as-a-second-language/),
  December 1, 2009; a talk delivered August 11, 2009. Supports plain vocabulary, active verbs,
  manageable sentences, and a human voice. The skill treats his opposition to inflated language
  as advice about reader effort, not an etymological ban or a ban on necessary technical terms.
- [Simple Geometry](https://theamericanscholar.org/simple-geometry/), April 23, 2010. Supports
  selecting a piece's essential parts and removing decoration that does not serve it.

The user's preference for concise, legible, precise prose guides the emphasis here. Safeguards
about code, evidence, measurements, and not inventing personal experience are practical adaptations
for an AI writing assistant. They are not presented as Zinsser quotations or book-specific rules.
